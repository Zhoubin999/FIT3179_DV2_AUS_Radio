Jargon buster

Weekly reach
The percentage of people who used something at least once in the past 7 days.
Share
Of all the time people spent listening to radio in a city, the percentage that went to one station.
Points
The simple difference between two percentages: 58% vs 50% is a gap of 8 points.
AM / FM
Traditional (analogue) radio, the kind every car radio can receive.
DAB+
Digital radio broadcast from towers. Clearer sound and more stations, but you need a DAB+ radio and a nearby transmitter.
Relay transmitter
A small transmitter that repeats a station from somewhere else so a far-away town can hear it (officially, a “retransmission”).
01

The big shift: streaming caught up with radio

Australians did not stop listening to radio. They added streaming and podcasts on top of it.

Streaming overtook radio in 2025, but radio is still heard by 3 in 4 people

Since 2017, weekly streaming rose from 46% to 81% and podcasts from 10% to 45%. Radio fell only slightly, from 85% to 77%. Digital DAB+ radio grew from 16% (2018) to 42%, so radio is moving to new devices rather than disappearing. The lines overlap because one person can do all of these in the same week.

Try: hover to compare every line in one year · click a legend item to highlight it · switch to Regional Australia in the menu below the chart.

Source: Infinite Dial Australia 2026 and Regional edition, people aged 10+.

Spotify is twice as popular as YouTube Music, and growth has stalled

Spotify is used weekly by almost half of Australians (47%). Apple Music and Amazon Music grew fastest in 2025, then stayed flat in 2026.

Try: hover a square to see the change from the year before.

Source: Infinite Dial Australia 2026, people aged 10+.

02

Who listens: age and location matter

Older listeners and people outside the capital cities rely on traditional radio more. Young people and city residents lead the move to digital.

Radio's youngest listeners are drifting away first

8 in 10 Australians aged 55+ listen to radio each week, in cities and regions alike. Among 10–24 year olds the gap is 10 points: 70% nationally but only 60% in regional areas.

A third of Australians now listen to and watch podcasts

Regional Australians use FM more; city residents use DAB+ more

58% of regional adults used FM radio in the past week, compared with 50% in the capital cities. For DAB+ digital radio it is the other way round: 14% in the cities and only 4% in regional areas. Chapter 3 shows why: DAB+ transmitters are mostly in the capital cities.

Try: hover a dot for the exact percentage.

Source: ACMA, How we watch and listen to content (2026), adults 18+, survey of June 2025.

03

Technology: new ways in, and the towers behind radio

Smartphones in cars and app subscriptions bring streaming into places radio used to own. Meanwhile, where you live decides which radio signals you can receive.

Phones are taking over the car dashboard

Apple CarPlay or Android Auto lets drivers stream from their phone through the car screen. Ownership rose from 10% in 2019 to 52% in 2026. In regional Australia it dropped from 47% to 40% in the last year. Each bar shows the change from the year before.

Try: hover a bar for the yearly change.

More than half of Spotify listeners pay for it

Each grid is 100 weekly Spotify listeners. 59 in 100 pay nationally, 56 in regional areas. Regional listeners are more likely to use someone else's account.

Digital radio is built for the big cities

There are only 29 DAB+ transmitter sites in Australia. Every capital has one big tower (20–50 kW), and Sydney and Melbourne add small “infill” sites to fill gaps. Outside the 11 regions shown here there is no DAB+ signal at all, which is why only 4% of regional adults use it.

Try: hover a region in the bar chart to find its sites on the map · hover a circle for the tower's address and power.

Circle size shows transmitter power in kilowatts (kW): more power means a signal that travels further. Source: ACMA list of licensed broadcast transmitters, July 2026.

Big, empty states need many small transmitters

The Northern Territory has 32.7 radio transmitter sites for every 100,000 people, compared with only 2.6 in Victoria. In Western Australia, 65% of FM transmitters are relays that repeat a station from elsewhere for distant towns. The ACT has the highest share of DAB+ sites (14%).

Try: use the menu below the map to switch between the three measures · hover a state for all its numbers.

Sources: ACMA transmitter list (July 2026) joined with ABS population, March 2026.

04

Local stations: what do city audiences choose?

Ratings surveys (GfK Surveys 3, 4 and 5 of 2026) show which stations people in the five largest cities listen to. Music stations take about half of all listening, but talk and news are far stronger in some cities than others.

Music rules everywhere, but Melbourne talks the most

Commercial music stations take 50–55% of listening in every city. Commercial talk and news ranges from 14% in Melbourne to just 4% in Brisbane. Since Survey 3, music has lost ground in Sydney (−2.8 points) and Brisbane (−4.1).
